The autopsy

Five ways the 2024 project was broken

Every finding below was verified by re-running the original code, bug-for-bug, against the same dataset (src/virtual_makeup/legacy.py in the repo).

1 · Wrong landmark topology

The “MediaPipe” pipeline detected a 468-point mesh, then indexed it with dlib’s 68-point region numbers.

lips = landmarks[48:60] # chin, on a 468-pt mesh

Effect: “lipstick” rendered as purple polygons across the chin and jaw.

2 · The blue-face save bug

Outputs were saved through an RGB→BGR conversion — on images that were already BGR.

cv2.imwrite(f, cv2.cvtColor(img, cv2.COLOR_RGB2BGR))

Effect: red and blue swapped across the whole photo. Every saved face was blue.

3 · The “GAN” was never trained

A 3-layer conv net with random weights ran predict() — there is no training loop anywhere in the notebook.

Effect: its “makeup” is noise by construction — and metrics were still reported for it.

4 · Destructive rendering

Opaque fillPoly lipstick over the whole mouth (teeth included), eyeshadow at fixed ±10/±20 pixel offsets, everything composited additively.

Effect: painted teeth, effects that fit exactly one image size, blown-out brightness.

5 · An evaluation that couldn’t fail

The reported “accuracy 0.88, precision 1.0” used a protocol where y_true is all ones.

Effect: precision is 1.0 for any output — including untrained noise. The headline metric was unfalsifiable.

Old pipelines vs the rewrite

Each method ran on the no-makeup half of the dataset the course project used. Colors are fixed per method across every chart.

Where the pigment landed

Share of edit energy inside legitimate makeup regions (lips, lids, lashes, cheeks). The rewrite’s remainder is invisible ±1-count feather falloff — at any non-zero mask threshold it is 1.0 by construction.

What the background suffered

Fraction of pixels outside the face left bit-identical. The blue-face bug recolors ~98% of the image; the untrained “GAN” replaces all of it.

Did lips still look like lips?

Correlation of lip-region lightness before → after. Flat fills erase pores and creases. (The 2024 MediaPipe row survives only because it usually missed the lips.)

The metric that couldn’t fail

The original evaluation: grayscale SSIM vs a reference photo, “accuracy” = share above 0.45. A completely blue face scores 0.527 — statistically identical to the best method — because grayscale SSIM is blind to color, and makeup is color. “Precision” was 1.0 for every row.
